The Last 48 Hours

Does the spread keep moving as kickoff approaches, and enough to justify refreshing picks more often than once a day at 10:00 ET? Here is what the stored data can and cannot answer — and the cheapest way to close the gap.

Not answerable yet

We own two snapshots per game, not seven

The paid archive bought a line ~4.5 days out and a line ~20 minutes out. Nothing sits at 48h, 24h, 12h, 6h, 2h or 1h. The curve you asked for does not exist in the database.

Strong circumstantial case

The move over that whole window is real and it pays

45% of games move ≥1 point between the two snapshots, 17% move ≥2. And the direction of the move predicts the result — monotonically, in both sports.

Act on this now

The 10:00 job is stalest exactly where the money is

Half of NFL games and 47% of CFB games are picked off a line more than 6 hours old — every 4:25 game, every prime-time game. Adding kickoff-anchored polls costs about 60 credits a week.

01 — the blocker

What you asked for, against what we actually bought

The 2026 archive acquisition — 10,180 credits for CFB, 9,390 for NFL — deliberately bought exactly two instants per game, because the strategy only needed two: a stand-in for the frozen CBS number and a stand-in for the line at submission time. It was the right purchase for that question. It is the wrong shape for this one.

A handful of CFB games do carry a near-kickoff first snapshot — the weekly oddsapi:frozen pull is taken at a fixed hour, so a Tuesday or Wednesday MACtion game gets caught only ~10 hours ahead. Seventy-four games sit inside 12 hours and another 63 between 24 and 48 — 137 of 3,717, essentially all weeknight and all thin-market. It is a hint, not a sample. Section 04 uses it as one.

03 — is the move signal or noise

Bigger late moves win more often. That is the whole argument.

Take the side the market moved toward between the two snapshots and grade it against the frozen line. If movement were noise this sits at 50% in every bucket. It does not.

This is why the timing question matters at all. We are not chasing volatility for its own sake; a bigger observed divergence is a better pick. Any movement that happens after our last read is edge we measured as valuable and then declined to capture. The open question is only how much of it happens late.

04 — three partial reads on the late window

What we can infer about the last 48 hours, honestly labelled

A. The weeknight natural experiment — suggestive, confounded

Because the weekly frozen pull happens at a fixed hour, its distance to kickoff varies by game. That accidentally gives us a crude dose-response curve: how much movement remains between a snapshot taken X hours out and kickoff.

Read literally, a snapshot taken 10 hours before kickoff still misses 0.70 points of movement on average, against 0.95 for one taken four days out. That would mean roughly three quarters of all pre-kickoff drift happens inside the final ten hours — a large claim, and one I do not want to lean on. The 10h group is entirely Tuesday and Wednesday weeknight games with 5–10 books quoting them; thin markets move more per hour than a Saturday marquee does. Mean absolute movement also does not decompose additively, so the arithmetic above is a heuristic, not an identity. Treat it as a reason to measure, not as the measurement.

B. The one week of real intraday polling we have — small, direct

CFB 2026 week 1 was polled 8–10 times per game against a real CBS board. That is the only place in the database where the actual question — does our pick change late? — can be asked end to end. Fifteen games:

  • 7 of 15 saw their divergence change at some point inside 48 hours.
  • 5 of 15 changed tier or acquired a side they did not have: Marshall–Penn State went coinflip → lean at T−5.5h; BC–Cincinnati went from an exact match with CBS (no side at all) to a home pick at T−5.5h; Oregon State–Houston picked up a side at T−27h; East Carolina–Alabama flickered coinflip → lean → coinflip between T−27h and T−26h; Washington State–Washington changed tier twice, landing back on lean only at T−6h.
  • 3 of those 5 changes landed inside the final six hours.
  • 0 of 15 flipped from one side to the other inside 48 hours. The late changes were tier moves and newly-acquired sides, not reversals — which fits the archive's 2.7% favorite-flip rate.

n = 15 and one week. But it is the right fifteen games, graded the right way, and it points the same direction as (A).

C. The countervailing fact — our consensus is deliberately sticky

Across those same 15 games, the median-of-11-books consensus barely budged between T−54h and T−5h: nine did not move at all, five stepped by exactly half a point, and one moved a full point. The median is a step function over half-point book quotes, and it is chosen precisely so one book cannot drag it. Individual books move far more than this line does. Any late-refresh policy will therefore capture less movement than the raw market shows — and the stat that matters is not "how much did the market move" but "how often did the consensus step". Section 02 answers that for the whole 4.5-day window — about 78% of games step at least once. How much of that stepping happens in the final 48 hours is precisely the number we do not have.

05 — the actionable finding

The 10:00 ET job is freshest for the games that need it least

Independent of how the market behaves, here is a fact about our schedule. For every regular-season game 2021–2025, how old was the line at kickoff if the last refresh was the 10:00 America/New_York job?

47.1% of CFB games and 49.1% of NFL games are picked off a line more than six hours old; 9.5% and 21.5% off one more than ten hours old. You do not need the movement curve to see that this is a bad shape. A single fixed wall-clock time cannot be close to kickoff for a slate that runs twelve hours. The fix is to anchor polls to kickoff rather than to the clock, and it is cheap: a live poll costs 1 credit and returns every game in the sport at once.

06 — closing the gap

Buying the answer costs more than we have. Collecting it costs almost nothing.

An archive snapshot is 10 credits; a live poll is 1. Both return every event in the sport at that instant, so cost scales with the number of distinct instants, not games. The balance on this key today is 17,919 credits.

07 — recommendation

Change the schedule now; let the data arrive as a byproduct

  1. Replace the fixed 10:00 refresh with kickoff-anchored polls

    Keep the 10:00 job as the daily digest, but drive poll-odds from the stored kickoff_utc values instead of the wall clock: for each distinct kickoff instant on the board, poll at T−12h, T−6h, T−2h and T−1h. The bot already discovers the current stored week on its own, so this is a scheduling change in discord_bot.py plus a new config block, not new pipeline machinery. lines is append-only and its primary key absorbs duplicates, so extra polls can only add history.

  2. Make the last poll before each kickoff a notification, not just a row

    The refresh only pays if it reaches you before you can still change a pick. Have the T−2h poll DM you only when it changes something that matters — a side flip, or a tier crossing 1.0 or 2.0. On the 2026 week 1 sample that would have fired on 4 of 15 games; a poll that changes nothing should stay silent.

  3. Re-run this report after five pool weeks

    At that point the oddsapi live source will hold four observations per boarded game, and every number in sections 02 through 04 can be recomputed at the offsets you actually asked about — on our slate, against real CBS lines, with pick churn measured directly rather than inferred. That is the point at which "should we refresh more often" stops being a judgement call.

  4. Do not buy the archive for this

    Revisit only if step 3 shows the late window matters and five weeks is too thin to size it. Then buy one design from the table above — NFL 2025 at four offsets, 5,020 credits — rather than the full grid.

One thing I could not settle from the code, and it changes step 1

All of the above assumes CBS locks each game at its own kickoff, so a late refresh can still change a Sunday-night pick after the 1:00pm games have started. If instead the whole board locks at the first kickoff of the week, then only one instant matters — T−1h before the earliest game — and the schedule collapses to a single poll per board, roughly 2 credits a week. That also caps the upside: the late games would still be picked 10–12 hours early no matter what we do. Which one is it?

Method & provenance

Source: data/pickem.duckdb, read-only, 10 Sep 2026. Consensus per game per snapshot is the median across books of each book's latest quote, matching pickem.edge.divergence.consensus_spread; spreads are home-perspective throughout. CFB population is sport='cfb', weeks 1–15 (the table holds no postseason), seasons 2021–2025 — every stored CFB game already has two FBS teams, since a game id is only constructed when both names resolve against the 136-school alias map. NFL population is weeks 1–17 for 2020 and 1–18 for 2021–2025, excluding the 13 postseason games per season. Games missing either proxy are dropped pairwise rather than imputed: 3,717 of 3,943 CFB and 1,585 of 1,615 NFL survive the join.

Hit rates in section 03 grade the market-implied side against the frozen snapshot's line using stored finals; pushes are excluded from the denominator. Line-age figures convert each kickoff to America/New_York and measure back to the most recent 10:00 local instant, so they include the DST shift.

Credit costs are computed as the count of distinct kickoff − offset instants for the stated population, at 10 credits per archive request and 1 per live poll. The balance of 17,919 was read from x-requests-remaining on /v4/sports, which is free. No paid request was made for this report.
